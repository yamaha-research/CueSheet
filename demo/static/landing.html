The concept

Crews already plan cues around these moments.
No existing system tracks them.

At a live concert, the production crew must anticipate every change of content to trigger audio, lighting, and video cues. The show-level segments they plan around (the lead-in, the songs, the talk between them, the applause, the break) are exactly what CueSheet detects.

Music structure analysis is offline

It assumes a finished recording and segments inside a song (verse, chorus, bridge). A live show needs labels while the audio is still arriving.

Score followers need a score

Real-time alignment tracks a written score, which most pop, jazz, and electronic concerts do not have.

CueSheet extends music structure analysis from song-level sections to concert-level segments, and runs it live.
The engineering idea

Every component is pretrained or hand-crafted.

A frozen AudioSet encoder produces 527-class posteriors each second. A fixed group mapping projects them onto four concert classes. An online causal HMM smooths them with only past audio, and two short structural rules add Pre-concert and Intermission. Steady-state latency is the 1-second hop.

The interface is AudioSet's 527 classes: any encoder that produces them drops in, so the pipeline improves whenever audio tagging does.

The taxonomy

Three acoustic events.
Three structural positions.

Performance

Music is playing. The AudioSet music subtree carries it.

MC talk

Spoken voice between songs: announcements, band intros.

Applause

Clapping, cheering, crowd response.

Pre-concert

The lead-in before the first sustained performance run.

Intermission

The sustained break between performance runs.

Ambient

The non-active residual.

The impact

CONCERT-10: open ground for the next system.

Released with the demo: 26 concerts, 22 hours, ten genres, as a URL manifest with per-second CueSheet-generated labels. We distribute links and annotations under CC BY 4.0, not the source media. Four shows carry dense hand-labeled ground truth for evaluation.

CueSheet segment track · one second per slice ·

For the multimedia community this opens a measurable task between song-level structure analysis and full production automation: concert-level segment detection, with a zero-training baseline to beat and applications in broadcast automation, archiving, and automatic chaptering.

At the demo

You drive it.

01

Scrub the gallery

Four hand-labeled concerts: pop, jazz, electronic, classical. Drag the playhead; the segment badge, posterior bars, and ribbon follow every second.

02

Swap the encoder

One click switches EfficientAT MN10, PANN CNN14, AST, or HTS-AT. The ribbon recomputes and each encoder shows its own measured wF1, inline.

03

Bring your own audio

Upload any recording, or just talk, play, and clap into the microphone. The same causal pipeline a venue deployment would run labels it live.

04

Export the chapters

Export the detected segments as YouTube-ready chapter timestamps or WebVTT with one click.
